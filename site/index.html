overview

your pitch becomes a real product

For the rest of the term your team builds, ships, and presents the full-stack AI application you pitched, the same way a professional software team works.

who are we presenting to?

the course staff is your client

Treat the instructor as your client and engineering leadership. Weekly consultations are your sprint reviews. Demo Day is where you show a working product to stakeholders and defend the decisions you made to get there.

By the end you should have a deployed product, a repository that tells the story of how it was built, and a presentation that proves you understand what you made.

choose-anything.md

you choose everything

Domain, framework, language, database, AI provider, host, and CI/CD tool are yours. There is no approved list. Be ready to justify every choice.

process-is-graded.md

process is graded

A polished app built in the last three days scores lower than a steady build with a visible history of planning, reviews, and deploys.

one-devops-engineer.md

one devops engineer

One teammate owns the CI/CD workflow and is graded on how well they design, automate, optimize, and manage it.

ship-continuously.md

you ship continuously

From Sprint 1 your app lives on the web, and every merge to main moves through an automated pipeline.

team

roles

Every team has exactly one DevOps Engineer. Everyone else is a Developer, and how you split development work is up to you. Declare roles in your README and at your first consultation.

devops-engineer.role

the devops engineer required

Owns the path from a developer's commit to a running production app: the pipeline, environments, secrets, deployments, and the numbers that show how fast and reliably the team ships.

They are not the team's backup coder. They may write application code, but their individual grade comes only from the CI/CD rubric.

product owner duty

One person, any role, keeps the backlog prioritized and speaks for the product at consultations. This can rotate weekly.

code review

Every change to main goes through a pull request reviewed by at least one teammate.

honest attribution

Commits, pull requests, and issue assignments are how contribution is measured. Work that isn't in the repository is hard to credit.

Roles can change once mid-project with a good reason. Tell the instructor first and document the change.

lifecycle

four sprints, one loop

Each one-week sprint ends with a consultation. The goals describe a healthy pace, not gates. If your project needs a different order, explain why at consultation.

  1. sprint planningPick the week's work from the backlog. Break it into issues of a day or two, each with an owner.
  2. build in branchesOne branch per issue. Work lands through a pull request that passes the pipeline and gets a teammate's review.
  3. async standupsTwo to three times a week in your team channel: done, next, blocked. Keep them as evidence.
  4. continuous deploymentMerged work deploys automatically. Production always reflects main.
  5. sprint reviewThe consultation: demo what's deployed, walk the board, present the pipeline report.
  6. retrospectiveA short written retro in your sprint log: what went well, what didn't, one change for next sprint.
done =merged to main through a reviewed pull request, passed the pipeline, deployed, and checked in the deployed environment. "Works on my machine" is not done.

devops track

build, run, and improve the pipeline

The DevOps Engineer is graded on one question: how well did you build, run, and improve the system that takes your team's code to production? Any CI/CD platform, any host. What's graded is the design, the automation, the measured improvement, and the evidence.

pipeline design

Check every pull request and deploy every merge to main with no manual steps.

environments

Production at minimum, ideally staging or per-PR previews, each with its own config and secrets.

secrets

Nothing secret enters the repo. Inject secrets from your CI or host's secret store, scoped per environment.

quality gates

Protected main branch, required checks, and required review before merge.

release and rollback

Know how to roll production back to the last good version, and prove you can.

measurement

Track pipeline performance weekly and back every optimization with numbers.

what should our pipeline look like?

a mature pipeline, for reference

This is a reference, not a required template. Build the stages your project needs, in an order that fails fast: cheap checks before expensive ones.

ai-specific.md
  • Cost. Mock the provider in unit tests; keep live calls to a small scheduled smoke test.
  • Prompts are code. When a prompt changes, run the eval set automatically and report whether quality held.
  • Non-determinism. Test the shape and validity of output, not exact wording.
  • Outages. A provider outage shouldn't block deploys of unrelated changes.
  • Key hygiene. Separate keys per environment, with spending limits on non-production keys.

grading

how it adds up

Team scores and an individual score combine, so a strong team can't carry an absent member and one bad sprint doesn't sink a strong individual.

30%Weekly consultations · team · 4 × 20 = 80 pts
40%Demo Day · team · 100 pts
30%Individual track · developer or CI/CD rubric · 100 pts

Each component becomes a percentage, then gets weighted. Example: 70/80 on consultations (87.5%), 85/100 on Demo Day, and 90/100 individually:

0.30 × 87.5 + 0.40 × 85 + 0.30 × 90 = 87.25%

faq

frequently asked questions

what to know before your first sprint.

can we use tools we didn't use in class?

Yes. Use whatever fits your product, and be ready to explain why you chose it over the alternatives.

can we use ai coding assistants?

Yes, the way professional teams do. You're still responsible for every line you merge. If you can't explain it at consultation or Demo Day, it counts against you.

can we change our project from the pitch?

Yes. Record what changed and why in your scope change log, and tell the instructor at your next consultation. Dropping one of the seven core requirements is not allowed.

does the devops engineer write app code?

They may, but their individual grade comes only from the CI/CD rubric. Developers shouldn't offload their own work onto them.

do we have to pay for hosting or ai?

No. The free tiers listed under hosting are enough for this project, and free or trial AI credits or a local model work too. Set spending limits on every key, and mock LLM calls in tests to keep CI costs near zero.

what if a teammate isn't contributing?

Raise it early with the instructor, not at Demo Day. The repository history and board make contribution visible, and individual scores reflect it.

what if our deploy breaks before a consultation?

Present it honestly: the incident, your diagnosis, and the fix or rollback. Handling a failure well is part of what's graded, especially for the DevOps Engineer.

what if production goes down on demo day?

Roll back to the last good release and keep going. A team that recovers calmly scores better than one that pretends nothing happened.
